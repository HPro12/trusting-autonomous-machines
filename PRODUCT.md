# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

General public, students, and policy-curious readers with no AV background. They arrive on a laptop or desktop browser, alone, with 10–15 minutes. The job: feel the "when should I trust a machine" problem in their hands before anyone explains it in words.

## Product Purpose

Trusting Autonomous Machines is an interactive public-interest education project about autonomous-driving safety. Each chapter is a self-contained HTML experience (no build step, no assets, no server) that puts the reader inside one hard question: human limits (Ch. 1), machine perception (Ch. 2), proving safety (Ch. 3). The chapters are the game component of a future website that wraps them. Success: a reader finishes a chapter and can articulate why "L2 ≠ autonomous" without having been lectured.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, grounded, sober. A night-drive documentary, not a quiz app. Real statistics carry the drama; the interface never celebrates or gamifies a crash.

## Anti-references

- SaaS landing-page grammar: hero metrics, feature cards, tracked-caps eyebrows as scaffolding.
- Gamified quiz aesthetics: points, confetti, streaks, "correct!" badges.
- Em-dash-heavy AI prose; generic "journey of discovery" copy.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't lecture.** The reader feels the 68 meters before reading the number.
2. **Diegetic UI.** HUD elements belong to the car; timestamps and locations are story, not chrome.
3. **Choices leave marks.** Every decision changes state the reader sees again at the debrief.
4. **Data is the drama.** Real reaction-time, takeover, and liability figures are the payload of every outcome.
5. **Portable by construction.** One HTML file per chapter, everything inlined, works from file://.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Full keyboard path (A/B/C choices, Enter/Space advance, hold-Space takeover, M mute). `prefers-reduced-motion` disables shake/grain/typewriter. Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast on the night palette. No flashing above 3 Hz.
