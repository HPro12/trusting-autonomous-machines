The machine · what it can actually do

Six levels. Only two share the road.

"Self-driving" isn't one thing. It's a ladder. Tonight you drive Level 2, hands off but eyes on. In the last chapter, you decide whether a city is ready for Level 4.

L0
No automation

You do everything. Wheel, pedals, eyes, all yours.

L1
Assistance

One thing at a time. Cruise holds speed, or lane-keep nudges the wheel. Not both.

L2
Partial

Steers and holds speed together, but you watch every second and take over instantly.

You drive this tonight
L3
Conditional

The car drives itself in limited conditions, and can hand back with little warning.

L4
High

No one at the wheel, but only inside mapped city zones. This is a robotaxi.

Chapter 5 decides on this
L5
Full

Drives anywhere a person could, in any conditions. Does not exist yet.

You don't read about it.
You drive it.

Ten to fifteen minutes, five chapters, one road. It won't try to convince you that self-driving cars are good or bad. It lets you feel why the question is hard, so you leave with better questions instead of a borrowed opinion.

Experience first

Feel it before you're told

Every idea arrives through something you do: a lane change, a sensor you have to trust, a verdict you have to sign. Never a paragraph you have to believe.

Your choices stay

The road remembers

What you decide follows you. The final chapter reads your whole path back to you: the trust you gave, and where it took you.

No verdict handed down

Better questions, not answers

The perspective changes each chapter; the world stays the same. By the end the goal isn't agreement. It's understanding why experts disagree.

Grounded, not guessed.

A public-interest education project, built on primary research rather than headlines.

  • Based on interviews with autonomous-driving professionals working in China: engineers, safety, and operations.
  • Reflects the legal and technical landscape as of 2026: what's deployed, what's assisted, and what the law actually says about fault.
  • Every statistic in the experience traces to a real source: reaction times, the miles needed to prove safety, the exposure gap.
  • The crashes and testimonies are dramatized; the numbers and the insights behind them are not.
Where we stand

Nowhere on purpose. This project takes no position on whether autonomous driving should be trusted. That's the one thing it deliberately refuses to do.

Instead it hands you the same uncertainty the people building and regulating these systems live with every day, and lets you sit in it. What you conclude is yours.

Trust the Machine

An interactive experience about when we should trust machines, and how much. Best on a laptop or desktop, with sound on. Controls live in the car: A/D or arrow keys to steer, M to mute, Space or Enter to advance.
